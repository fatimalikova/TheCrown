Ephemeral

/ɪˈfem.ər.əl/ • Adjective
Lex. Cod. 1409 local_florist
❖ Azərbaycan Dili Tərcüməsi translate

Müvəqqəti, qısaömürlü, tez keçən

Təbiətdə və ya duyğularda bir göz qırpımında yox olan təravət.

Scholarly Explication:

Lasting for only a very short period of time; fleeting, transitory, or of brief, evanescent duration; reminiscent of dayflies whose lifespan encompasses but a single cycle of the sun.

Exemplum Literarium
“The ephemeral beauty of fallen autumn leaves adrift upon moist garden flagstones...”

Tərcümə: Payız yarpaqlarının nəm daşlar üzərində uçuşan müvəqqəti gözəlliyi.

account_tree
Etymon Roots

Ancient Greek ἐφήμερος (ephēmeros), from epi (upon) + hēmera (day) — literally “lasting only a single solar day.”

Liber Primus • Pars Aestiva ❧ ❦ ☙ Fol. XLVII
Caput Quartum

The Enchanted Garden

It was an hour past dusk when Julian ventured across the overgrown flagstones, guided solely by a strange sense of serendipity that had seemed to accompany his strides through the forgotten county of Sussex. The air hung thick with damp moss, crushed elderberries, and that peculiar, lingering melancholy which visits grand estates fallen from grace.

He had learned in his youth to look forward to these solitary rambles, for within them time ceased its tyrannical march. Yet tonight, the moon cast long, skeletal rib-cages of shadow through the yew arbor, evoking an ephemeral radiance upon the sundial that would dissolve the instant clouds swept overhead.

A soft rustle perturbed the laurel bushes. Julian paused, startled by the silhouette of Lady Beatrice standing near the ruined fountain. Neither had spoken since the winter solstice ball, and he wondered how one might break the ice without shattering the solemn sanctity of the night.

“You promised the rector you would carry out the manuscript survey before Michaelmas,” she whispered, her voice crisp as frost on winter thistle. Her words lacked malice, but Julian knew the fleeting nature of her temperament, as unpredictable and swift as the tides of the Solent.
